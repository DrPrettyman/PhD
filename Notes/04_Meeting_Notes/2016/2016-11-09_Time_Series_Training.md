# Time Series Analysis in Environmental Science - Postgraduate training program

The Training program consists of two lectures each morning followed in
the afternoons by practical sessions relating to those lectures.\
The course "Time series analysis in a changing Climate" given by Shane
Elipot of the University of Miami is very interesting. It is based on a
series of lectures given by Jonathan Lilly found at
`www.jmlilly.net/talks/oslo/index.html`. The slides for this lecture are
found at

- `rsmas.miami.edu/users/selipot/` or

- `https://selipot.github.io/talks/`

In an off hand way, Elipot mentioned that the two principal semi-diurnal
tidal "species" have period of 12 h ($M_2$) and 12.4206012 h ($S_2$).
This is an interesting fact to note.\
Many topics relating to spectral analysis were discussed. In particular
the **Multi-taper method** for estimating the Power Spectrum of a
series. This is apparently much better than the "periodogram"
($\frac{1}{N}\Delta_t|\texttt{fft}(x)|^2$), which is all I have ever
known. For identifying periods this may be true, it is not so clear that
there will be a huge improvement in the estimation of the scaling
exponent (slope of the log-log plot) but there will certainly be some
improvement.\
Shane Elipot also gave all students a (free to use and download) matlab
toolbox 'JLab', developed by himself and J. Lilly, which was used in the
practical session. The toolbox contains many time series and spectral
analysis tools, including the afore-mentioned multi-taper method for the
power spectrum.\
The course "Time series in methane measurement and analysis" (Giuditta
Marinaro and Mariagrazia De Caro) focussed on the use of very specific
(Python and C) tools developed by the speakers to look at a specific
time series of methane measurements in the Marmara sea. No general
discussion of time series analysis.\

# Time Series Analysis in Environmental Science - Conference

Quick notes:

- lots of ocean data available from the Fixed-Point Open Ocean
  Observation project, `www.fixO3.eu`.

- Somebody is talking about multi-variate data, they use the Hotelling's
  T$^2$.

- The Summer Maximum and Winter Minimum sea ice cover are both
  declining, but the min is declining faster, so a greater area of ice
  is freezing each year. However, the ice is possibly less thick than it
  was so in terms of volume, probably less ice is freezing each year.

- The talk by Davide Faranda (and Dimitri Defrance who was not there)
  was very interesting. About comparing real life systems to the
  "Lorentz butterfly", calculating the fractal dimension of a patch of
  the observation space as an indicator of how rare the event is.

- Davide also cited an IPCC report about extreme climate change and
  risk: http://jech.bmj.com/content/66/9/759.short

- Davide has apparently published very many papers with Valerio
  Lucarini, and also has written a chapter for the book *Extremes and
  Recurrence in Dynamical Systems*, for which Tobias also wrote a
  chapter.

- Paul Wilkinson from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine
  told the conference that the ideal temperature for someone from
  Yorkshire is 16.1$^oC$. This is the temperature where there are fewest
  deaths, controlling for other factors. For London the temperature is
  20.5$^oC$.

# Comment on the conference

There was too much focus on results and not on methods. Most speakers
wanted just to talk about their interesting results but did not go into
detail about their methods. Apart from Davide Faranda I did not find
anything interesting or new, that is, nothing that could not be done
using an in-built MatLab function. Possibly the results are new, or the
ways of collecting data, going on ships into the Arctic ocean and using
cutting-edge sediment-sampling techniques, but I would not know.

# Comment on Scientists

I found the talk of climate change to be very depressing. The scientists
seem very cheery about the end of the world because it will be a good
opportunity to do more science. Cathrine Lund Myhre (NILU) gave a talk
about the compositional change of the atmosphere, she mentioned that the
Fukashima disaster was a very great experiment for her team as they
tracked the movement of the gasses. She seemed very positive about such
a tragedy. Even though many speakers discussed climate change, melting
sea ice, and one speaker even gave a talk about declining fish stocks in
the North Atlantic, nobody seemed to mind eating copious amounts of fish
and meat at every meal. It seems that to be a scientist one must
separate one's self from the subject and become an outside observer -
this made me feel quite uncomfortable, possibly because I am not used to
it.\
During the conference Donald Trump became president elect of the USA.
There were many jokes, but also concern that funding for climate science
may decrease - no direct concern that climate-unfriendly policies may
hasten the deaths of millions.

# Comment on Tromsø

Tromsø is a very nice city and is very typically Norwegian. I was able
to see the Northern Lights on Wednesday and Thursday nights. On Friday
night a "Northern Lights Tour" was organised at a cost of £70 each using
an established company. I had hoped we would be driven to wherever had
the best clear-sky forecast since it was a cloudy night, but we were
only taken to a few well-known destinations where nothing much could be
seen. Instead, one should take the cable car to the top of the mountain.
Even when there are no lights there is an amazing view of the city.\
The Arctic Cathedral is a very beautiful modern building. By chance I
was walking past at exactly midnight on Thursday and there happened to
be a "Midnight Concert" of Norwegian folk music and classical pieces,
this was very beautiful and the highlight of my tourist trip.
