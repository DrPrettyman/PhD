    		\begin{block}{Introduction}
    		
    		Many physical systems are governed by dynamics that permit more than one state, such as the glacial/interglacial cycle of our climate. Moreover, a bifurcation in the dynamics may change the number of permitted states. \\
    		\vspace{1cm}
    		A dramatic and relatively sudden shift from one state to another is known as a tipping point. Hopefully the time series from a measurement of the system will reveal the tipping point. 
		
    		\end{block}
    		
    		\begin{block}{Project Aims}
    		There are two possible goals whose paths of approach are intertwined:
    		\vspace{1cm}
    			\begin{enumerate}
    			\setlength{\itemindent}{3em}
    			\item Detecting a tipping point
    			\item Predicting a future tipping point
    			\end{enumerate}
    		\vspace{1cm}
    		This PhD project aims to develop a multi-purpose prediction tool for tipping points in physical systems. Several techniques are used to analyse time series for Early Warning Signals. \\
    		\vspace{0.5cm}
    		These techniques are also applied to toy models in order to understand their limitations. 
    		\end{block}
    		
    \end{column}

    \begin{block}{A Simple Example of a Tipping Point}
    \begin{columns}[T]
    		\begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
    Equation (\ref{eqn1}) describes a stochastic dynamical system $z(t)$ in the form of a potential function with added noise $\eta_t$. The shape of the potential function changes as $t$ increases (see figure, right), removing the stable mode at $z=0$ and creating a double well potential.
    \vspace{0.5cm}
\begin{align}
\dot{z}(t) & = -\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z^4 + \left(3-\frac{t}{200}\right)z^2) + \eta_t
\label{eqn1}
\end{align}
\vspace{0.5cm}

The $\eta_t$ are iid Gaussian. At $t=600$ the $z^2$ term is zero and the bifurcation occurs. This is seen in the time series plot below, a model run with $z(0)=0$.

    		\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
    			\begin{block}{Tipping Point Prediction: Fingerprinting}
    			It is proposed that a a tipping point visible in a time series will be anticipated by a rise in the series' autocorrelation [1,2], as a forcing pushes the system towards the tipping point.
    			To detect a rise in autocorrelation we calculate possible indicators in a sliding window of $\sim N/5$ points.
    			\vspace{1cm}
    		\begin{columns}[T]
    		\begin{column}{0.02\textwidth}
			\end{column} 
			\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
			\begin{figure}
			\includegraphics[width = \textwidth]{one_and_many_ACF.png}
			
			\caption{\label{ACF_all} The ACF lag-1 estimator is calculated for 100 model runs upto the point of bifurcation ($t=600$). }
			\end{figure}				
			\end{column} 
			\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
			\begin{figure}
			\includegraphics[width = \textwidth]{method_comparison.png}
			\caption{\label{comparison} A comparison of the three autocorrelation indicators discussed. In each case the indicators are calculated for all 100 series, using a widow of 200 points, and then the mean of all is taken as in Figure \ref{ACF_all}. The scaling exponent and DFA coefficient have been scaled linearly to fit on the same axis.}
			\end{figure}			
				
			\end{column}
			\begin{column}{0.02\textwidth}
			\end{column} 
		\end{columns}
		\vspace{1cm}
		
		In Figure \ref{comparison} we see that all indicators begin to rise at $t\approx 400$, at $t=500$ an upward trend is clear. In this case, all three provide a good EWS. In many situations we may not be able to take the mean of 100 experiments, certainly for predicting a geophysical event we will have only one time series. In Figure \ref{ACF_all} we see that the ACF signal is not so clear in this case, it is thus worth considering all indicators.

    			\end{block}

    		\begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
    			\begin{block}{Multi-dimensional systems}
    			There is the potential for data to be multidimensional in various ways: that several variables are recorded; and also that measurements are made at many points in space, giving an array of time series. There is likely to be a trade-off between having enough data to ensure a reliable Early Warning Signal, and too much noise.\\
    			\vspace{0.5cm}
    			\begin{columns}[T]
    			\begin{column}{0.05\textwidth}
    			\end{column}
    			\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
    			It is common in geophysical applications to reduce the dimension of a system using Empirical Orthogonal Functions [1,2]. Taking the ``first EOF'' we project a time series onto coordinates such that the variance is maximised. It is assumed that this captures the ``interesting'' aspects of the system.\\
    			\vspace{0.5cm}
    			We have studied the system 
    			\begin{align*}
    			\mathbf{x}_{t} = B(t)\mathbf{x}_{t-1} + S\eta_t
    			\end{align*}
    			to test the conditions under which the EOF assumption may fail. The system either spirals to zero, or diverges, depending on the eigenvalues of $B$. It is unclear whether these states are represented by the EOF.
    			\vspace{0.5cm}
    			\begin{figure}
    			\includegraphics[width = 0.8\textwidth]{spiral.png}
    			\caption{In this case the eigenvectors of $B$ are in the direction $(3,1)$ and $(-6,1)$}
    			\end{figure} 
    			
    			\end{column}
    			\vrule
    			\begin{column}{0.02\textwidth}
    	   		\end{column}
    			\begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
    			It is also possible to consider several dimensions at once. In [6], a two-dimensional analogue of the ACF estimator ([1]) is formulated. The method is applied to the system 
    			\begin{align*}
    			\dot{x} = y,~~~~\dot{y}=\mu(t)-x^2
    			\end{align*}
    			where $\mu$ is made to vary linearly from $0.5$ to zero. At zero ($t=100$) the system experiences a homoclinic bifurcation.
    			
    			\vspace{0.5cm}
    			\begin{figure}
    			\includegraphics[width = 0.8\textwidth]{Williamson.png}
    			\caption{It is hoped that the eigenvalues of the ``2-D ACF'' provide an Early Warning Signal.}
    			\end{figure} 
    			\vspace{0.5cm}
    			Note the imaginary part of the first eigenvalue decreases as the tipping point approaches. It is not clear that this method will work in general.

    	   		\begin{block}{Potential Analysis: Detection of Tipping Points}
    	   		One aim of tipping point research is detection. One approach (as in [5]) is to assume that a system resembles a $n$-well potential system, with a form similar to Equation (\ref{eqn1}), and to fit such an equation to a window of the time series.

    	   		\begin{column}{0.02\textwidth}
    	   		\end{column}
    	   			\begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}

							\end{figure}
    	   			\end{column}

    	   			\end{column}
    	   		\begin{column}{0.02\textwidth}
    	   		\end{column}
    	   		\end{columns}

			The figures (taken from [5]) show Antarctic ice-core temperature proxy data (left) and a contour plot showing the estimated number of potential wells of the best-fitting model: red - one; green - two; cyan - three; purple - four wells. The shape of the fitted potential function depends on the window size ($y$-axis of contour). This example does not show a clear bifurcation (as in the Equation (\ref{eqn1}) example) but a patchy pattern. Livina et  al. [5] show that further knowledge of a physical system my be necessary.

   		\begin{column}{0.32\textwidth}
   			\begin{block}{Building a model of Tropical Storms} 
   			We suggest that tipping point analysis could be used to predict the approach of tropical storms, the ACF indicators of sea-level pressure (slp) or wind speed may behave similarly to those of a bifurcating system (eqn.1). This is shown to be the case in preliminary tests using slp, as shown in the figure:
   			\begin{figure}
   			\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Katrina.png}
   			\end{figure}
   			However, this is not so clear for most storms. Complicating the analysis is noise from unrelated weather events and bi-daily (tidal) oscillations in slp. We propose creating a stochastic model which includes these features of the form
   			\begin{align*}
\frac{dx}{dt} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x} U(x,t) + A_1(x,t)  + A_2(x,t) + T(x,t) + \eta
			\end{align*} 
			using the Unscented Kalman Filter to parametrise ([8]). By studying this model we hope to understand the dynamics and in which ways they are sensitive to tipping point analysis.
   			\end{block}
